**The Idea in Between**

When I first listened to Glenn Gould’s *The Idea of North*, I was
floored. I knew him as a virtuoso pianist, but I had not fully grasped
the depth of his engagement with experimental media. The piece arranges
and composes interviews alongside sound. Voices enter clearly, then
begin to overlap, interrupt, and blur. At moments the listening becomes
difficult. You cannot hold or discern every word. And yet that
difficulty is precisely what opens space for thought. The work invites
listening and resists it at the same time. It creates a sonic
environment where ideas emerge through the experience of difficult
listening rather than straightforward explanation.

Gould’s decision to move away from live performance and into the studio
allowed for this exploration. It signaled a shift from spectacle to
construction, from a privileging of the audience and its experience to
arguing for a mediated intimacy that would create something else for
both performer and listener. As someone who has played music in front of
live audiences and as someone who can’t imagine not seeing live shows
Gould’s legacy is all the more powerful to think through and engage
with.

What, as artists, do we want? What as listener’s and consumers of art do
we want? What is possible?

This dialogic project attempts to work through some of these questions
and provocations.

The initial thought was to create a narrative conversation between
thinkers who I have been inspired by in one way or another. But thinking
about the dialogic and its possibilities I see this as a first draft.

This text exists as a simple and minimal website.It is a script, a
score, an outline of a possible audio/media work. The website format
matters because it helps to provide a structured framework. The main
text is centered on the site with the 4 texts surrounding it. The main
text itself was going to originally be more of a back and forth dialogue
between the thinkers. But taking inspiration from Gould I have decided
to share uninterrupted and free flowing dialouge from each thinker.
These texts would then serve as soundtracks to be woven together and
played together, simulataneoulsy. Each “track” would have moments of
audio clarity, played alone, but then at times tracks would come
together and almost resemble a one on one dialogue and the again all the
tracks would be played at the same time, voices competing against one
another and the listener not knowing exactly whom to listen to. In
addition, I have begun to think about spatial cues and sonic soundscapes
that might emerge and accompany the individual and group voices.

White space functions like pause. In this sense, the website can be seen
as a conceptual space to explore and play with ideas. It is a space
where I can experiment with how the thinkers and words relate to one
another before complicating and making this a more immersive and media
based experience.

The theories I am engaging are not secondary to this text. They shape
its form. The conversation between Martin and Buber is one that I have
had for years in my own mind and with a few close friends. For me, Agnes
Martin is one of the great pure artists. Any chance I get I see her
work. To sit and stare at one of her pieces is to sit beside her in
silent contemplation. Her mysticism and chosing to spend time alone is
what makes her work so special. Yet, she shared her work in galleries
for others to enjoy and experience. Her claims about inspiration only
coming from within seem to be in opposition to Martin Buber’s writing
about they I-Thou encounter. I began reading Martin Buber at almost the
same time as I first learned about the work of Agnes Martin. It is
remarkable that they most probably never met, maybe didn’t even know
about one another, yet spatially they might have been in close proximity
to one another in NYC during the 50s. His claim that “all real living is
meeting” pushes me to think about Agnes Martin’s life and existence. Her
choosing to seemingly live and create outside of “meeting” with others.
How then can a media project begin to explore an encounter between these
two individuals. To further explore the ideas of interiority and
exteriority I thought an additional dialouge between more contemporary
artists/thinkers would be interesting, especially ones that have
explored more media related work such as video and audio. Bringing
together Derek Jarman and Trinh T. Minh-Ha as a second conversation that
explores the solitary but also solidarity seemed like an interesting
transition to the present moment.

**Website** The website was built using Codespaces in Github. Through
numerous prompts I asked Codespaces to develop a minimal and simple
website with the texts provided.

**Setting:** The sun is rising across the desert landscape in Taos, NM.
The time does not matter. A lone figure, Martin Buber, slowly approaches
a small ranch home. On the porch of the home sits a women in a chair.
The air is clear and the only sounds you can hear are the foosteps of
Buber approaching the home and the chair creaking in which the women
sits.

There is a long silent moment once Buber stops about 10 feet from the
porch. He is facing Martin but her chair is at at slight angle so it
prevents direct eye contact.

This scene feels like it can go on forever but there is a dramatic cut
to flowers growing wildly in a interior courtyard. Derek Jarman and
Trinh T. Minh-Ha sit together on a swinging bench inches from the
flowers. The clouds above them move furiously across the sky blocking
and making openings for the sun.

The dialogue begins with a lone voice but throughout the soundscape
weaves in and out from one voice to all speakers alongside additional
ambiet noises and soundscapes.

**Agnes Martin Text:**

The desert air is life. It lifts the sun and lets the colors shine
brightly. Some think that I came out to the desert to find refuge and
escape from New York City, but in fact, the desert called for me. It saw
me for me. It gave me a new community. The quiet and open spaces filled
me with a joy, caused vibrations that would find their way through my
brushes and onto the canvas. A new way to connect with and speak to
other people.

“All people have access to two parts of their mind”

The world appears before us as a series of transactions, of facts, of
determinations, of the day to day that brings us rest, exhaustion,
solitude, friendship, hunger, and all of the multiplicities of the
physical. Our outerminds record and help us get through this landscape
of existence. The inner mind is there as well. Waiting for you to make a
decision about *doing*. It is in this doing in which we can find joy,
elation, or inspiration. Inspiration exists in the mind. It is not in
the world of objects.

Inspiration creates us. It reinvents. It pushes us into the mystery that
is life.

People speak of the desert as empty. It is not empty. It is exact. It
does not interfere. It does not impose narrative. It allows you to see
without desire. Beauty is not in the object seen. “Beauty is the mystery
of life.” It is not in the eye, it is in the mind. It is not something
you possess. It happens when you are absent enough to receive it.

I paint lines because they are not things. A line does not describe. It
does not dominate. It does not wound. It allows the mind to rest in
perfection.

“Happiness is being on the beam with life.”

What is life in this context? Is not life everything? To be on the beam
is to find balance, to constantly align ourselves with life. To be in
life. To live.

I do not divide the world. I divide attention. When attention is given
to inspiration, the outer ceases to dominate. There is no conflict in
the desert. Only clarity.

The painting is not about the desert. It is about innocence. It is about
the moment when thought ceases to interfere. When you see without
wanting to own what you see. It is not a transaction.

If I sit in silence, it is because silence allows the inner mind to be
heard. Silence is not absence. It is the fullness of our thoughts.

**Martin Buber Text:**

Twofold. The world exists twofold. Our attitudes exist twofold. It takes
two people.

When we experience life and to understand it we do so through two
relationships: I–It and I–Thou.

When you sit with It or with Thou do you see a difference?

There may be an inner and outer mind. But I speak of relation. The I
that says “It” is not the same I that says “Thou.” When I say “It,” I
experience, I measure, I use. The world becomes an object among objects.
Existence is transactional. The cup is to be filled with water, but for
whom? Yet, I can stand before a tree and enter into a relation with it.

“if I have both will and grace, that in considering the tree I become
bound up in relation to it. The tree is now no longer *It*.”

When I say Thou, I do not experience something wholly inside me. I is
only possible because of Thou. Now in this experiece we find our ethical
self.

*All real living is meeting*

Other experiences may be profound but it is only through the Thou,
through this meeting between presence that we experience something true
about the Universe and our place in it.

The desert may appear empty. But emptiness does not open the door to
this meeting, this real living relation. One can stand before the
horizon and still remain in I–It, analyzing color, light, temperature.
The Thou is not found by abstraction, nor by withdrawal alone. It is
found in presence.

Some say beauty is in the mind. To them I say: relation is not in the
mind alone. It happens between two people. It must exist in that mutual
space.

The Thou cannot be grasped. The moment one tries to fix it, it becomes
It. Even God, whom I have called the Eternal Thou, cannot be possessed.
Only addressed.

The danger of perfection is that it tempts us toward solitude without a
response to the Thou.

“In the beginning is relation.”

Not isolation. Not transcendence beyond encounter.

When you paint or create, do you address? Whom do you address? Does the
other dissolve and become an It?

Yet, is silence indifference? The desert does not answer, yet it
receives. Perhaps relation does not always require speech, does not
require the physical presence of an other. But does it require searching
within yourself first, a willingness to find oneself first? I say that
you become yourself through and with another presence.

Yet, I can’t help but think that without the Thou, the I itself becomes
mere function. It cannot venture towards inspiration.

**Derek Jarman Text:**

I planted a garden when I saw that my end was coming. In Dungeness,
where the wind tore everything sideways. The garden was not a retreat;
it was a life. It became my life.

Even as my visions turned blue from the medications that could never
save me I found my inspiration by the sea. With my sight dissolving and
AIDS taking my body I saw that the blue could remain.

“Blue is the universal love in which man bathes - it is the terrestial
paradise”

The land and our connection with it provides resistance. My landscape
was dying, just like me, salt air, illness. Yet there, inspiration
insisted to show itself brightly and for me.

The body was made to betray, to die one day and almost never by our own
choosing. Our vision clouds, it is a cloud, obscuring and revealing all
that we might see. But color persists beyond my body. Beyond what we can
see.

Silence is always brimming with life, few of us have ever really
experienced true silence. Silence accentuates the echos from the voices
within hospital corridors, the humming of machines that keep us alive.
Blue was and is a space between life and disappearance. To grasp it is
to feel it evaporating from your fingertips.

In the courtyard here, I imagine plants pressing upward despite the heat
from the sun. They go towards the light even if it will burn and dry
them up.

“In time no one will remember our work.”

That is what illness teaches.Ephemerality. But in the present tense of
seeing and doing, something holds.

Is blue a Thou? Or is it the last It before darkness?

I seek to share myself. A mark against erasure. Even if that mark
dissolves into blue.

**Trinh T. Minh-Ha Text:**

“To speak nearby is not to speak about.”

It is not to seize the object and fix it in language. It is to maintain
a relation that does not colonize.

In the descriptions and translations of the desert, I listen for the
risk of purity. Landscape has often been made innocent by those who can
afford innocence. Representation is never neutral. What is it about the
universe that lets you or me enjoy this moment when others are left
outside in the cold?

The gaze frames your existence. It excludes as much as it inspires.
Every articulation produces a silence elsewhere. Can you still hear that
silence? Can you give it voice?

The Thou and my relationship to others still remains imperfect. If it
were only so easy. Who has the authority to address? Which\_ I’s\_ get
to experience other *Thous* truly. Who decides Thou? It seems that *I’s*
too often reduce *Thou’s,* as if we have yet to learn to speak *with* as
opposed to speak *for* or *against*.

I resist the urge for my voice to speak for someone. I seek the
silences, the gaps, and the hesitations that allow for others to be
heard. The unsaid is a strategy for listening and being heard. It can
sometimes say more than all of the words we have in our languages.

“To speak nearby, is a great challenge . . . It is an attitude in life,
a way of positioning oneself in relation to the world.”

It is proximity without totality.

There is beauty of immersing oneself fully into the blue. But does
immersion erase difference? Perhaps? But it may also make us hyper aware
of our own positionality and mortality even within beauty.

Listening is never passive. Listening is labor. Listening is love.

Perhaps this porch and the courtyard, all spaces, are not separate but
in fact linked and intimately tied together through overlapping
acoustics. In this sense, how can we better understand our relationship
with others? How do we prioritize and situate ourselves to others?

The ethical demand is not silence, nor speech, but attentiveness to the
space between.
